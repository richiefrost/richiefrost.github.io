Background

Where this comes from

Dropbox

2022 – 2026 · ML inference platform

I worked on the platform that runs ML models at Dropbox: multi-tenant inference on Kubernetes, on Dropbox's own hardware. The job was making it efficient, available, and scalable enough that teams across the company would rather use it than pay a vendor.

That meant a lot of time on latency, GPU utilization, tenant isolation, and autoscaling, plus convincing engineers to actually adopt the thing. It paid off: [cut AI spend by X%] with [N models / teams] running on the platform.

Microsoft

2019 – 2021 · ML & data science

I built fraud detection models that turned up millions of dollars in recoverable funds, along with the production pipelines behind them. Those pipelines took the time to get an audit prediction from a month down to 30 minutes.

A lot of the work was statistics: figuring out whether a number is real before anyone makes a decision on it. That habit carries into everything I do now, especially when it comes to judging whether an AI feature is actually working.

Questions

Things people usually ask

Do I need to be technical to work with you?

No. Plenty of people with fast-built apps aren't engineers, and that's fine. I'll explain what I find in plain English and be clear about what actually matters versus what can wait.

Should we be hosting our own models?

Probably not yet. Self-hosting paid off at Dropbox because of the scale. For most teams, the bigger and faster wins come from using the APIs better. If you're at the point where running your own models makes sense, I'll show you the math.

What do you work in?

Mostly Python, Kubernetes, and the major clouds, along with the usual model providers and open-weight models. If your app is built on something else I can usually still help, and I'll tell you upfront if I can't.

Will you sign an NDA?

Yes. Send yours over.

Can you just tell us what to do, without doing it yourself?

Sure. The reviews are designed to be useful on their own. Some teams take the write-up and run with it, and that's a perfectly good outcome.

Tell me what's going on.

Thirty minutes, no charge, no pitch deck. Worst case, you leave with a second opinion.
